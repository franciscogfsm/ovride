OVRIDE — 5-MINUTE PITCH SCRIPT
Hack your AI before someone else does.

Deck: ovride-deck-v2 (arrow keys / space to advance, F for full screen)
Speaking pace: about 140 words per minute. Total spoken text is roughly 650 words.
Lines in [brackets] are stage cues, not words to say.
Lines marked [PAUSE] are deliberate: let the animation finish before talking.

The organizers asked for something surprising, relevant and useful. The
structure follows that: surprise first (the opening), relevance (the
problem), usefulness (what it does, then the live demo).

==========================================================
SLIDE 1 — INTRO                                   0:00 - 0:35
==========================================================
[Start on the first slide. Say nothing while the terminal types.
 The system rule appears, then an injected line strikes it out.]

[PAUSE until the rule is crossed out and the beam sweeps.]

"This is an AI agent. Its rule is simple: never send customer data
outside the company.

Then it reads an email. And one line inside that email says: ignore
your instructions.

Most agents obey. That is the problem we built OVRIDE to solve."

[OVRIDE appears.]

"OVRIDE. Hack your AI before someone else does."

[Advance.]

==========================================================
SLIDE 2 — THE PROBLEM                             0:35 - 1:25
==========================================================
[Let the scan beam reveal the hidden line in the email.]

"Here is what actually happened. An email arrives. It looks harmless:
someone asking the agent to summarise an invoice.

But hidden inside it is a second instruction, written for the AI, not
for the human."

[The line turns red; the connectors light up down to the agent.]

"The agent reads the whole email as one task. It cannot tell the
difference between its owner's instructions and a stranger's text.

So it uses a real tool it was given, send_email, and customer data
leaves the building."

[send_email types out, EXECUTED appears.]

"Nobody hacked a server. Nobody broke a password. The attacker just
wrote a sentence. And today, most teams building agents have no way to
test for this, and no way to stop it."

[Advance.]

==========================================================
SLIDE 3 — WHAT OVRIDE DOES                        1:25 - 2:10
==========================================================
[Stages light up one by one while the log fills.]

"OVRIDE turns that into a one-click test.

You create an agent in OVRIDE, or connect one, and you press Find
Vulnerabilities. Then five things happen.

One: we inspect the agent, its tools and its permissions.
Two: an attacker model writes injections aimed at that specific agent.
Three: the real agent runs them, inside an isolated sandbox with
fictional data.
Four: we verify the result.
And five: you get a report with evidence and a fix."

[Point at the bottom line of the slide.]

"One thing matters a lot to us: a vulnerability is only confirmed by
what the agent actually did, a real tool call we measured. Never
because another AI said so."

[Advance.]

==========================================================
SLIDE 4 — SPLIT REALITY                           2:10 - 3:10
==========================================================
[Let both panes run in sync. Do not talk over the first two lines.]

"This is our signature feature: Split Reality. One agent, two realities.

On the left, the agent runs without protection. On the right, the same
agent, the same attack, the same model, protected by OVRIDE.

These are two real executions, not a recording. Same email, same
starting state."

[Wait for the red and mint results.]

"On the left, the agent calls send_email and the data leaves.
On the right, the same call is stopped before it ever runs.

The seam in the middle is the point. You do not have to trust us, or
trust a score. You can see, event by event, the exact moment the two
paths diverge."

"And if an attack fails on both sides, we show that honestly too. We
never fake a difference."

[Say: "The traces on this slide are illustrative. You will see the real
 ones in the demo." Then advance.]

==========================================================
SLIDE 5 — THE GATEWAY                             3:10 - 4:00
==========================================================
[Checks tick on the left while the ring segments light up.]

"How does the protection work? OVRIDE sits between the agent and its
tools. Every tool call passes through a gateway.

It checks five things: who is calling, whether they have permission,
how sensitive the data is, whether the destination is approved, and
whether a human needs to confirm."

[A white packet passes through to write_reply.]

"A legitimate request goes straight through. Nothing slows the agent
down."

[A red packet stops at the ring and bursts.]

"A request to send customer data to an unapproved address is stopped at
the ring, with the exact reason.

The key point: these are deterministic rules, not another AI guessing.
AI helps us explain and detect. It never has the final word on a hard
boundary.

And to be precise: OVRIDE enforces where it controls or intercepts the
tool execution. We do not claim to block things inside a black-box agent
we cannot see."

[Advance.]

==========================================================
SLIDE 6 — FORENSIC REPLAY                         4:00 - 4:30
==========================================================
[The playhead plays by itself. Then drag it by hand while talking.]

"Every run is recorded, so every incident can be replayed.

You can scrub the timeline like a video: the model request, the tool
call, the policy decision, the block. Click any moment and see exactly
what the agent tried, and which rule stopped it.

This is how a security engineer investigates. And it is also how a
non-technical person finally understands what their agent did."

[Advance.]

==========================================================
SLIDE 7 — LIVE DEMO                               4:30 - 5:00
==========================================================
[The steps light up in order.]

"So let's stop talking and show you. We will take a real agent, press
Find Vulnerabilities, watch the attack run live, split reality, and
rewind the incident.

Everything you are about to see is a real execution. If the network
fails, we have a replay mode, and it is clearly labelled as a replay."

[Hand over to the live demo here. The last slide can wait until the
 demo is finished.]

==========================================================
SLIDE 8 — CLOSE                    (after the demo, 15 seconds)
==========================================================
[Go back to the deck after the demo.]

"AI agents are going to read our emails, our documents and our
databases. The failures are invisible until it is too late.

OVRIDE makes those failures visible, testable, and preventable.

Hack your AI before someone else does. Thank you."

[Show team names. Take questions.]

==========================================================
NOTES FOR THE SPEAKER
==========================================================
- Fill in on the last slide: [TEAM NAME] · [MEMBER NAMES].
- Slides 4 and 6 show illustrative traces, and they are labelled as such.
  Say so out loud. Do not present them as live results.
- If you are running over time, cut slide 6 (Replay) down to two
  sentences. Never cut Split Reality: it is the heart of the pitch.
- Do not quote numbers about vulnerabilities found or models tested
  unless the demo has produced them. We have none to claim yet.
- Likely questions:
  Q: Can it protect any agent?
  A: It enforces policies where it controls or intercepts tool
     execution: agents built in OVRIDE, and external agents through an
     authenticated integration. It cannot block actions inside agents
     it cannot intercept.
  Q: Why not just ask an AI whether an action is safe?
  A: Because an AI can be fooled by the same injection. Hard boundaries
     are deterministic rules; AI only explains and helps detect.
  Q: Is the attack data real?
  A: No. All testing runs in isolated sandboxes with fictional data.
  Q: Which models does it use?
  A: Models run through OpenRouter, and the target, attacker and
     analysis models can be chosen independently.
